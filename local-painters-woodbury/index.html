Prepare for a useful first conversation

Before you call or arrange a visit, make a quick list of the areas you want to discuss. Note any known surface problems, previous repairs, or changes you'd like to make. If you have paint colors in mind, share them, but it's fine to begin with a general idea. Mention practical details too, such as parking, entry instructions, or rooms that are occupied during the day.

You can use this checklist:

  • Identify each room or exterior area you want considered.
  • Write down visible concerns, including peeling, stains, or damaged spots.
  • Decide which spaces need to remain accessible during the work.
  • Gather color ideas or photos that help explain your preference.
  • Ask what furniture, decor, or belongings you should move beforehand.
  • Confirm the business hours and the best way to arrange an estimate.

The more clearly you describe the job, the easier it is to discuss what is and isn't included. If you're weighing proposals, compare their listed work and preparation details rather than relying on a short summary. Ask about any item you can't interpret. Clear answers help you plan without making assumptions about the contractor's responsibilities.

Plan around your household and the work area

Painting can affect how you use a room, so think about your routine before setting a date. Consider whether you work from home, have children or pets, or need access to a particular doorway. Tell the contractor about those needs while discussing the project. You can also ask how to protect personal belongings and what areas should be kept clear.

For work outside, point out the surfaces you want reviewed and any access limitations around the home. Woodbury's changing seasons can affect when exterior work is practical, so ask the painter how current conditions may factor into scheduling. Don't assume a date will suit every surface or project. Discuss timing directly and leave room for the contractor to assess the work.

Common hiring mistakes to avoid

Don't leave the scope open-ended. A request to paint a room may mean different things to different people, so identify the surfaces you expect to be part of the job. Don't wait until the work is underway to bring up repairs, color changes, or extra areas. And avoid clearing out your schedule without first discussing access and expected timing.
