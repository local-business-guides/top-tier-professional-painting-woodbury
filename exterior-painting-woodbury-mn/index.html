What should exterior painting include?

Exterior painting is a sequence of preparation and coating work, not just a fresh color on the walls. The agreed scope may cover siding, trim, fascia, soffits, doors, shutters, or other painted surfaces, so confirm which areas are included and which are not.

A thorough plan accounts for the surface material and the condition of the existing finish. It typically includes cleaning, allowing surfaces to dry, removing loose paint, smoothing rough edges, making agreed minor repairs, and priming bare or repaired areas when needed. The finish coats should suit the substrate and the existing coating.

Ask how the crew will protect windows, landscaping, walkways, and outdoor fixtures. A written scope should also explain how the painter will handle areas that need more repair than expected, so there is a chance to agree on the work before it changes.

What does a local professional check before recommending a plan?

A painter should look closely at siding and trim rather than estimate the whole job from a quick glance at the front of the house. Peeling, chalking, cracks, soft or swollen wood, failed caulk, and stains can all point to different preparation needs. If there are signs of ongoing moisture, the cause should be addressed instead of simply covered with paint.

The inspection should also identify the surface materials, how much of the old coating is sound, and whether access will be difficult around rooflines, porches, or landscaping. In Woodbury, the seasonal changes make it sensible to plan around suitable temperatures and dry conditions, rather than rushing application between rain or cold spells.

A useful estimate describes the preparation and surfaces included, notes visible damage, and explains any assumptions. Ask the painter to point out what is included in the quoted scope and what would need separate approval if uncovered during the work.

What changes the cost of exterior painting?

The price depends on the amount of work required, so two homes with similar square footage can have very different estimates. A larger paintable area, multiple stories, steep or difficult access, detailed trim, and many doors or other separate surfaces can all add labor.

Surface condition matters just as much. Extensive scraping, sanding, caulking, priming, or repairs can take more time than painting sound surfaces with limited preparation. Material type, the number of colors, the coating selected, and how many coats the project needs may also affect the total.

To compare estimates fairly, check that each one covers the same surfaces and preparation. Ask whether cleanup, protection, primer, minor repairs, and a final walkthrough are included, and request written approval for additional work if damage is found. A low quote can be hard to compare if important steps are left out.

How can weather and scheduling affect the project?

The calendar duration includes more than the time spent applying paint. Washing and repairs need time, and surfaces must be dry before many coatings can be applied. Rain, damp conditions, temperature, and the product's directions can affect when the crew can start or continue.

Ask how the painter plans to handle weather delays and whether the schedule is an estimate or a firm start date. Also ask how the crew will keep the job site safe and protected if work pauses between stages. In a Minnesota season, a realistic plan is better than a promise to finish quickly regardless of conditions.

What mistakes should homeowners avoid?

  • Choosing a quote based only on the total without checking the included preparation and surfaces.
  • Painting over peeling or poorly bonded coating instead of asking how it will be removed or stabilized.
  • Ignoring soft wood, leaks, or other signs of moisture and expecting paint to fix the underlying issue.
  • Failing to agree on color, sheen, trim, doors, and any excluded areas before work begins.
  • Assuming every repair is included, even when the estimate only covers minor preparation.
  • Scheduling without asking how rain or unsuitable temperatures could affect the work.

If a home has widespread peeling, damaged wood, or a coating that may need special handling, ask for an in-person assessment before agreeing to a simple repaint. A professional can explain whether painting is appropriate or if repairs or further evaluation should come first.

Questions to ask before hiring, a checklist

  • Which siding, trim, doors, and other surfaces are included in the estimate?
  • What cleaning, scraping, sanding, caulking, and priming are included?
  • What paint or coating do you recommend for these materials, and why?
  • How will you protect windows, plants, walkways, and outdoor fixtures?
  • Who will be doing the work, and who should I contact with questions?
  • How do you document and price repairs that are not visible before work starts?
  • What is the expected schedule, and how do weather delays affect it?
  • What cleanup, walkthrough, and written warranty or touch-up terms are included?

Clear answers make it easier to compare providers and avoid different assumptions about the job. Keep the estimate and any changes in writing, including the colors and surfaces you approve.
