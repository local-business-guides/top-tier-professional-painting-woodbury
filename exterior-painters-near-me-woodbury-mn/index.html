What should an exterior painting project include?

A well-planned exterior painting project begins with a review of the surfaces and their condition. The painter should identify what will be painted, note areas with peeling or failed coating, and explain which preparation steps are needed before applying new paint.

The written scope should name the surfaces included and describe washing, scraping, sanding, caulking, priming, paint application, and cleanup as they apply to the home. It should also explain how windows, plants, walkways, outdoor fixtures, and nearby surfaces will be protected. Ask the painter to separate included repairs from work that would require approval or a separate estimate.

What will a local painter check before giving an estimate?

A professional should look closely at the siding, trim, doors, and other surfaces in the proposed scope. The material, existing coating, and amount of peeling or damage can change the preparation plan. Look for a clear explanation of any soft, cracked, or weathered areas, rather than a quick estimate based only on the home’s size.

The visit is also a chance to discuss access, landscaping, and the areas you want left untouched. On a Woodbury home, ask how the painter will account for exposure to sun, rain, and seasonal temperature changes when choosing a work window and planning the application. The inspection should leave you understanding what is known and what may need a closer look once loose paint is removed.

What changes the cost of exterior painting?

There is no reliable price to give without seeing the home and defining the work. Cost depends on the size and number of surfaces, their condition, height and access, the amount of preparation, and whether repairs are included. Trim details, doors, porches, and other separate surfaces can also add work.

The products and application plan matter as well, especially when different materials or conditions call for different primers or coatings. Ask each painter to identify what is included, what is excluded, and how added work will be priced and approved before it begins. That makes it easier to compare estimates without assuming that the lowest total covers the same job.

How can weather and scheduling affect the project?

Exterior painting depends on suitable conditions for the specific paint and surface, so the schedule can shift with rain, temperature, moisture, and drying time. A careful painter should explain how weather affects the planned work and how schedule changes will be communicated, rather than promising a fixed finish date regardless of conditions.

Ask when the crew expects to start, how many workdays are anticipated, and whether those days are consecutive or weather-dependent. Also agree on access to the yard, doors, and parking, and ask how outdoor furniture or plants should be handled before work begins.

What mistakes should homeowners avoid?

  • Choosing from the total alone without checking whether the bids cover the same surfaces and preparation.
  • Accepting a verbal promise instead of a written description of the work, products, cleanup, and repair limits.
  • Assuming all damaged wood, siding, or trim will be repaired as part of painting. Ask what repair work is included and how unexpected conditions will be handled.
  • Skipping questions about protection for landscaping, windows, walkways, and outdoor fixtures.
  • Treating reviews as a substitute for checking current availability, insurance, references, and the terms of the agreement.

A painter may uncover conditions that were hidden by loose paint or were not visible during the first visit. Agree in advance that additional work will be explained and approved before it changes the scope or cost.
