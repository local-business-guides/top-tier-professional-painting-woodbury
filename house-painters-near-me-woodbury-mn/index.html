What should a house painting service cover?

A painting service should start with a conversation about which rooms or exterior surfaces are included and what condition they are in. Before work begins, you and the provider should agree on the preparation, paint application, protection, and cleanup that the job requires.

  • A written list of the rooms, walls, trim, doors, or exterior areas included.
  • The preparation plan, including cleaning, sanding, patching, caulking, and priming where needed.
  • The paint finish and color choices, plus who is supplying the paint.
  • How floors, furniture, landscaping, and nearby surfaces will be protected.
  • What cleanup looks like and how any excluded repairs or extra work will be handled.

Ask for the scope in writing, not just a general description such as “paint the main floor.” That makes it easier to see what is included and discuss any changes before they affect the schedule or final bill.

What does a local professional check before giving an estimate?

A useful estimate comes after someone has looked closely at the surfaces and asked about your expectations. A professional may check for peeling or loose paint, stains, cracks, moisture signs, rough patches, and previous repairs. The right preparation depends on what is found, so a quick look from across the room may not be enough for a detailed scope.

The walkthrough should also clarify access and protection needs. For example, tall or awkward areas, rooms with a lot of furnishings, and exterior surfaces near plants or walkways can take additional planning. Point out concerns such as recurring stains or damage rather than assuming a fresh coat will solve them.

Ask the estimator to explain which issues are part of the proposed work and which would need a separate repair. If the cause of a stain or peeling paint is uncertain, the estimate should not promise that paint alone will prevent it from returning.

What changes the cost of house painting?

There is no reliable way to estimate a project from the word “painting” alone. The price depends on the amount of work and the condition of the surfaces, as well as the products and preparation included in the proposal.

  • How many rooms or exterior surfaces are included, and how large or tall they are.
  • Whether surfaces need extensive cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, caulking, or primer.
  • The number of colors, the finish selected, and any specialty coatings or detailed trim work.
  • How much furniture, landscaping, or nearby material needs to be protected or moved.
  • Whether repairs are part of the painting scope or need a separate trade or estimate.

When reviewing estimates, check that each provider is pricing the same areas and similar preparation. Ask what could change the quoted scope, and request approval before added work begins. That is more useful than comparing bottom-line amounts that may cover different tasks.

How can the project schedule change?

Timing depends on the size of the job, the preparation required, the number of people assigned, and how many coats or drying periods the chosen products call for. Access matters too: a room that needs to be cleared or an area that cannot be reached safely may affect the sequence of work.

For exterior work, weather and the product's application conditions can affect when painting can proceed. Ask the provider how they handle a forecast that interrupts the plan, and whether the estimated schedule includes preparation and cleanup as well as painting.

Before booking, confirm the expected start window, the estimated work period, and what you need to do before the crew arrives. If your plans depend on finishing by a particular date, say so early and ask whether that timing is realistic.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing only by the lowest quote without checking what preparation and cleanup it includes.
  • Assuming every surface in a room or on an exterior is included when the estimate does not name it.
  • Leaving questions about paint, finish, colors, or repairs until the crew is ready to start.
  • Accepting a verbal change to the scope without confirming the added work and any effect on timing.
  • Overlooking recurring moisture, peeling, or stains that may need attention before repainting.

Call a professional when the project involves difficult-to-reach areas, widespread peeling, substantial surface damage, or a problem that keeps returning after repainting. A painter can explain what preparation is appropriate and whether a separate repair or inspection should happen first.

If your home is part of an association, check its color or exterior-change requirements before choosing a color or setting a start date. Approval requirements can vary by property and project scope, so confirm any local requirements that apply before work begins.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which rooms or surfaces are included, and what is specifically excluded?
  • What preparation do you expect each surface to need?
  • Which paint and finish are included, and who supplies them?
  • How will floors, furnishings, landscaping, and nearby surfaces be protected?
  • Who will be on site, and who should I contact with questions?
  • What is the expected start window and work schedule, including drying time?
  • How will additional repairs or changes to the scope be priced and approved?
  • What cleanup is included, and what should I do before the crew arrives?

A provider's answers should be specific enough that you can compare them with another proposal. If something important is unclear, ask for the estimate to be revised before you agree to the work.
