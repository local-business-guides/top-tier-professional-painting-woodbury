What should stucco painting include?

A complete proposal should describe surface preparation, minor repairs included in the work, protection for nearby windows and landscaping, the coating system, and cleanup. It should also say what is excluded, such as major stucco reconstruction or fixing the source of water intrusion.

Stucco can have a textured finish, small cracks, or areas that absorb paint differently. The painter should examine the surface and explain whether washing, scraping, patching, sealing, or a primer is appropriate. The right steps depend on the condition and prior coatings.

What should a local painter check before quoting?

A useful estimate starts with a look at the full area, not just a quick calculation from the home's size. The painter should note cracking, peeling, powdery paint, patched spots, staining, soft areas, and places where water may be entering.

They should also ask about previous paint or repairs and identify access needs, nearby plants, and surfaces that need covering. If there are signs of moisture or significant stucco damage, painting alone may not solve the problem. Ask whether the estimate assumes those areas will be repaired by someone else.

For homes in Woodbury, weather and seasonal conditions can affect when exterior work can be done. A painter should explain how temperature, rain, and drying time affect the schedule rather than promising a start or finish date without checking the forecast.

What changes the cost and timing?

The price depends on the size and height of the area, how much preparation is needed, the amount of repair work, access, and the coating products specified. A sound, previously painted surface usually calls for a different scope than stucco with loose coatings, extensive cracking, or signs of water damage.

Timing also depends on surface cleaning and drying, repair and primer requirements, the number of coats, crew availability, and weather. Ask the painter to separate the work sequence from the estimated calendar time, and to explain how rain or unsuitable temperatures could change the schedule. Get the scope in writing so you can compare bids on the same basis.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing only by the lowest quote without checking which preparation steps and repairs are included.
  • Assuming paint will stop leaks or correct loose, damaged stucco. Find out whether the underlying issue needs separate repair.
  • Skipping questions about primer, coating type, number of coats, and how cracks or patched areas will be treated.
  • Accepting a vague verbal promise instead of a written scope, cleanup plan, and explanation of excluded work.
  • Booking exterior work without asking how the painter handles rain, drying time, and weather delays.

When should you call a professional?

Call a painter for an on-site assessment when you see widespread peeling, recurring cracks, chalking, staining, or uneven patches, or when you are unsure what coating is already on the wall. A professional can explain what appears to be paint preparation and what may need repair before painting.

If stucco is soft, visibly separating, or repeatedly damp, ask about the source of the problem before authorizing a paint-only job. The painter may recommend a qualified repair professional for work outside the painting scope. This helps prevent paying for a finish that cannot address the underlying condition.

Stucco painter questions to ask before hiring (checklist)

  • Have you assessed the stucco in person, and what condition issues did you find?
  • What cleaning, scraping, patching, sealing, or priming does your written scope include?
  • What coating do you propose, and why is it suitable for this surface?
  • How many coats are included, and what areas or repairs are excluded?
  • Who will do the work and supervise the job?
  • How will you protect windows, plants, walkways, and nearby surfaces?
  • How do weather and drying conditions affect the schedule?
  • What local permits, registration, or other requirements apply to this specific work?
